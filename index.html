John Huang (Huang Tung Yee)

I'm an undergraduate in Mathematics with an Extended Major in Artificial Intelligence at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST, Class of 2029). This site collects my work, notes, and the things I'm learning along the way.

Most recently I spent a summer at the Academy of Mathematics and System Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences, where I helped develop and validate the CAX integrated algorithm platform — turning mathematical thinking into software that runs. Before that, my background was mostly competition mathematics: an ICAS Principal's Award and High Distinction, and a Bronze in the AIMO senior-secondary semi-final. You can find the fuller list on the About page.

Beyond coursework, I've chased mathematics and science wherever they led — a summer class on differential geometry, the China Aerospace Education Program, and the Astronomical Training Programme for secondary students. Life & Notes is my space for personal writing.

I work comfortably in C and Python, and I enjoy helping other students find their footing through the HKUST Science Peer Mentor Program. If you'd like to talk — maths, code, or anything in between — the easiest way is the Contact page.

In Vibe research idea, I'm outlining possible directions in spectral graph algorithms, streaming algorithms, and computational learning theory, alongside exploratory AI questions. These are research proposals and aspirations.

I'm also collecting my course notes in multivariable calculus, linear algebra, interest theory, and macroeconomics — with worked examples, formula references, and PDF versions for revision.

Explore Can You Hear the Shape of a Drum?, an interactive mathematics essay about vibration, geometry, and the surprising limits of what a spectrum can tell us. Play with membrane modes, predict how size changes frequency, and discover an isospectral pair.

Practise with 積分研習室 · Integral Lab, an independent Integration Bee training space with all 66 publicly archived MIT 2026 season problems and four original variations. Work through progressive hints, check your answers, and try timed practice.
