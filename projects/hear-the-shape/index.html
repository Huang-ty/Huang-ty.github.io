What does the Laplacian measure?

Freeze time and view displacement as a height map U(x,y). A first derivative measures slope; a second derivative measures how that slope changes. The two spatial contributions add.

ΔU = Uxx + Uyy ≈ (four neighbor values − 4 center values) / h²

The four-neighbor formula explains the local comparison on an equally spaced Cartesian grid. The solver here instead uses triangular finite elements. For U=x²+y², ΔU=4; for U=x²−y², ΔU=0 despite curvature in both directions. Zero Laplacian at a point does not mean a flat surface.

Why are the Dirichlet eigenvalues positive?

Multiply the eigenvalue equation by φ, integrate, and use integration by parts. The boundary term vanishes because φ=0 on the fixed edge.

λ∫φ² = ∫|∇φ|²; λ = spatial variation / squared size > 0

This is the Rayleigh quotient for an eigenfunction. The denominator is positive. A zero numerator would make φ constant on this connected domain; the boundary then forces the excluded zero function. It also explains why multiplying a mode by any nonzero amplitude does not change λ.

Why does a matrix eigenvalue problem appear?

Test the differential equation against a function w that also vanishes on the boundary. Integration by parts lowers the derivative order. Substitute φ_h=ΣvⱼNⱼ and take w=Nᵢ.

∫∇w·∇φ = λ∫wφ → ΣKᵢⱼvⱼ = λΣMᵢⱼvⱼ
Kᵢⱼ = ∫∇Nᵢ·∇Nⱼ; Mᵢⱼ = ∫NᵢNⱼ

The program uses a row-sum lumped mass matrix: each triangle of area A contributes A/3 to each vertex. Boundary nodal values are fixed to zero and eliminated. Refining the mesh changes the approximation space; a small matrix residual alone is not a bound on the continuum error.

WHY TWO DIFFERENT PATTERNS CAN SHARE A NOTE

The spectrum counts independent directions.

Eλ = eigenspace of λ; multiplicity m(λ) = dimension of Eλ

On a square of side L, the (1,2) and (2,1) patterns both have λ=5π²/L². They are independent, so this value appears twice in the spectrum. Any nonzero combination αφ₁₂+βφ₂₁ has that same eigenvalue. Its nodal lines can look different without creating a new frequency. The computed square modes provide one possible basis.

What is the program actually solving?

We solve −Δφ = λφ inside the polygon, with φ = 0 on its outer boundary. A conforming triangle mesh and piecewise linear finite elements turn this into K v = λ M v. We approximate M by row-sum mass lumping, then use a block eigensolver to obtain six low eigenpairs, retaining independent vectors at repeated eigenvalues.

On each little triangle, the height is interpolated linearly from its three vertex values. Fixing all boundary values to zero makes the whole outside edge stay still. K measures the cost of spatial variation, while M measures the size of the displacement pattern: Kᵢⱼ = ∫Ω ∇Nᵢ · ∇Nⱼ and Mᵢⱼ = ∫Ω NᵢNⱼ. The Nᵢ are the local basis functions. This is the link from a differential equation to a matrix eigenvalue problem.

A time-dependent mode is u(x,y,t) = φ(x,y) cos(c√λ t), so f = c√λ/(2π). The colors show this displacement; the dark interior curves mark approximate nodal lines, which remain still. At an instant when the whole mode passes through zero, these curves still identify its permanent nodes.

For sound we use f_demo = 220√(λ/(1.25π²)) Hz. We do not retune each shape’s lowest mode to the same note: changing the outline or its size can change its pitch. The visual rate uses the same eigenvalue ratio, with the reference slowed to 0.42 cycles per second. A pure tone isolates one frequency; a real strike usually excites a mixture of modes with different amplitudes and decay.

A small algebraic residual checks the discrete eigenproblem; it does not measure the difference from the continuum drum. Refine the mesh to compare estimates. At a repeated eigenvalue the plotted basis is not unique: independent mixtures can be equally valid modes. Matching six numbers is not a proof that two shapes are isospectral.

Derive the patterns and their frequencies, step by step

Start with two masses: why eigenvectors?

For two equal masses joined by three equal springs with fixed ends, q″=−αK_toy q, where α is spring stiffness divided by mass. A motion q(t)=a(t)v preserves one spatial pattern only if K_toy v=μv.

K_toy = [[2,−1],[−1,2]]; (1,1) has eigenvalue 1, and (1,−1) has eigenvalue 3.

In either pattern, the acceleration remains along the same vector direction. The time equation becomes a″+αμa=0, giving a frequency ratio √3, rather than 3. On a continuous membrane, the vector becomes a function and the matrix becomes a differential operator.

Separate space from time.

Uniform tension 𝒯 and surface mass density ρ give c²=𝒯/ρ in the small-amplitude model. For a nonzero single mode, try u=φa. Time derivatives act on a; spatial derivatives act on φ. Substitution gives the first identity below. Away from zeros, separation makes the two sides of the second identity constant; the final equations avoid division at nodes.

φa″ = c²aΔφ; a″/(c²a) = Δφ/φ = −λ

Thus −Δφ=λφ and a″+c²λa=0. A nonzero motion also requires φ=0 on the fixed boundary.

Let the fixed ends select the spatial wavelengths.

On 0<x<a, X(x)=A sin(kx)+B cos(kx). The condition X(0)=0 gives B=0. Then X(a)=0 forces ka=mπ, with m a positive integer. Apply the same argument in y.

φmn = sin(mπx/a) sin(nπy/b), m,n positive integers

Differentiate twice to identify the eigenvalue.

Each derivative in x supplies a factor mπ/a; the second supplies a minus sign. The y derivative works the same way. Add the two contributions and compare with −Δφ=λφ.

φxx = −(mπ/a)²φ; φyy = −(nπ/b)²φ; λmn = π²(m²/a²+n²/b²)

Convert restoring strength into frequency.

Verify a(t)=cos(ωt): differentiating twice gives a″=−ω²a. Comparing the time equations produces the square root. Angular frequency and Hz differ by 2π.

ω² = c²λ; T = 2π/ω; f = c√λ/(2π)

Here the sound uses one common illustrative calibration, 220√(λ/(1.25π²)) Hz. Volume scales amplitude, not λ. The animation uses the same ratios with a much slower clock.

Read permanent nodes from the spatial factor.

Interior nodal lines satisfy φ=0 for the entire motion: x=ka/m or y=ℓb/n, with k=1,…,m−1 and ℓ=1,…,n−1. When the time factor is momentarily zero, every point crosses the resting plane; that instant does not make every point a node.

Separation of variables for a membrane.

A nodal line has zero displacement throughout the motion. Neighboring regions can move in opposite directions. A real strike usually excites several modes at once; the motion is their superposition. Changing the outline changes which patterns fit the boundary.

With the physical conditions fixed, each frequency corresponds to an eigenvalue, proportional to frequency squared. The complete eigenvalue list, including repeats, is the spectrum.

03

THE SPECTRUM ALREADY KNOWS SOMETHING

You can hear the area.

The full frequency list contains more than a lowest note. At high frequencies, the rate at which modes accumulate reveals the drumhead’s area. For smooth boundaries and ordinary polygons, the complete spectrum also determines the perimeter. These are exact facts about the ideal model, not estimates from an everyday recording. [4]

So the question is tempting: if the spectrum knows that much, might it know the whole outline? The study of these connections between geometry and eigenvalues is called spectral geometry.

THE MATHEMATICS / INCREASE THE CUTOFF

Count modes, then take a limit.

N(Λ) = number of eigenvalues ≤ Λ, with multiplicity

Λ is the cutoff you choose. N counts independent modes; two independent modes at one eigenvalue count twice.

N(Λ)/Λ → A/(4π); finite estimate Aest = 4πN(Λ)/Λ

Weyl’s law concerns the growth rate at large cutoffs. The finite number displayed by the experiment is an estimate, not an exact area formula.

We use Weyl’s law: area ≈ 4π × (number of eigenvalues ≤ Λ) / Λ. It becomes exact in the limit Λ → ∞. Finite estimates fluctuate and contain boundary effects; they need not improve at every step. Both shapes use the same length units and wave speed.

Why the constant 4π appears: count a quarter ellipse

Turn the rectangular spectrum into a counting problem.

Every positive integer pair (m,n) labels a mode. Keeping eigenvalues up to Λ is equivalent to keeping lattice points inside a quarter ellipse in the mode-index plane, not on the physical drumhead.

λmn≤Λ ⇔ m²/a²+n²/b²≤Λ/π²

Compare the number of points with the ellipse area.

The semiaxes are a√Λ/π and b√Λ/π. The quarter-ellipse area grows like Λ; boundary counting error for a fixed rectangle grows at most like √Λ. Divide by Λ and the relative boundary contribution vanishes.

N(Λ) = abΛ/(4π) + O(√Λ)
A = 4π lim N(Λ)/Λ

This gives a derivation for rectangles. Weyl’s theorem extends the leading law to the bounded smooth domains and ordinary polygons considered here. Local fluctuations remain possible at finite cutoffs.

Kac [4]. These two equal-area rectangles are not isospectral.

ONE MORE SPECTRAL INVARIANT / PERIMETER

The boundary leaves the next term.

Heat trace Z(τ) = Σe^(−τλj) = A/(4πτ) − L/(8√(πτ)) + O(1)

The complete spectrum determines Z. Its leading coefficient gives area A; the next gives perimeter L. Here τ is the heat parameter with length² units, not the vibrating membrane’s time in seconds. Smooth boundaries and ordinary polygons share these first two terms; corners contribute at constant order.

Derive the boundary term and recover its coefficient

Let the same spatial mode diffuse instead of oscillate.

For the heat equation vτ=Δv, a spatial eigenfunction decays as e^(−τλ)φ. Summing these modal decay weights defines the heat trace. It depends only on the eigenvalues, rather than on one chosen initial temperature distribution.

v = e^(−τλ)φ; Z(τ) = Σe^(−τλj)

Read the area term and the boundary correction.

The plane’s heat-kernel diagonal is 1/(4πτ), giving A/(4πτ) over the interior. Near a straight zero-temperature boundary, an opposite mirror source removes e^(−d²/τ)/(4πτ) at distance d from the edge. Integrating this loss perpendicular to each unit of boundary gives:

Boundary loss per unit length = 1/(8√(πτ))

This is an exact half-plane calculation and a local explanation of the general coefficient; controlling a curved boundary and the remainder requires heat-kernel analysis.

Extract the coefficients using only the full spectrum.

First isolate area with a limit. Then subtract the area term before reading the next coefficient.

A = lim 4πτZ(τ); L = 8√π lim √τ[A/(4πτ)−Z(τ)]

Six low eigenvalues cannot determine these small-τ limits. The heat expansion also does not, by itself, establish a universal two-term counting-function formula. All constants here use the positive operator −Δ.

THE SURPRISE

Different shapes.
The same spectrum.

Mark Kac popularized the question in his 1966 essay. In 1992, Gordon, Webb and Wolpert published different planar drumheads with the same complete spectrum. Even an infinite frequency list need not determine one outline. [1]

The pair below comes from a later account by Buser and colleagues. Each region is built from seven congruent scalene triangles. Their outlines cannot be matched by moving, rotating or reflecting one onto the other. [2]

THE MATHEMATICS / COMPARE THE TWO SHAPES

The forward map is not one-to-one.

S(Ω) = (λ₁(Ω), λ₂(Ω), …), including multiplicities
WHAT THE COUNTEREXAMPLE ESTABLISHES
A and B are not congruent; their complete spectra agree.

Two genuinely different domains have the same image under the spectral map. This disproves general uniqueness.

A NECESSARY CONDITION, NOT A REVERSE TEST
Equal spectra imply equal area and perimeter.

Equal area or identical construction pieces do not establish equal spectra. Geometric non-congruence and spectral equality need separate arguments.

THE MATHEMATICS / SELECT A TRANSPLANTATION RULE

Move functions, then use linearity.

T maps a λ-eigenfunction on A to a λ-eigenfunction on B.

The inputs are whole function pieces, each geometrically aligned to the target triangle. The signs tell you how to combine their values. A valid transplantation must preserve the equation inside, the seams between pieces and the zero outer boundary.

Mathematical tools: isometries · linear operators · odd reflection · eigenspace dimension

How the proof works: transplant a vibration pattern+

Take any mode on drum A. Call its seven function pieces f₀,…,f₆. To build a mode on B, align reflected copies of selected pieces and add or subtract their values. Choose a target triangle to inspect its rule. [2]

g₀ = f₁ + f₂ + f₄

Align pieces 1, 2 and 4 with target triangle 0, then add their values point by point.

THE SELECTED RULE, WITH ITS COORDINATE MAPS
g₀(x) = f₁(τ₀₁(x))+f₂(τ₀₂(x))+f₄(τ₀₄(x))
−Δg₀ = ΣⱼT₀ⱼ[(−Δfⱼ)∘τ₀ⱼ] = λg₀

Each τ maps the target triangle isometrically to the indicated source triangle. All source pieces have the same λ.

Green + adds a function; rust − subtracts it. These colors identify terms, not displacement. Each symbol stands for a whole function after geometric alignment, not a number or a loose physical tile.

  1. Keep the same eigenvalue. Reflection preserves the Laplacian. Linearity lets aligned pieces with the same λ be combined.
  2. Make the pieces fit. The particular rules match across internal seams and make the sum vanish at the fixed outer edge. A minus sign represents a sign-reversed reflected function.
  3. Account for every mode. The invertible correspondence acts on each λ-eigenspace, preserving its dimension. This gives equal eigenvalues and multiplicities throughout the spectrum.

This is a guide to the published proof, not a simulated eigenmode or a proof from a few matching frequencies. Arbitrary triangle rearrangements do not satisfy these rules. Read Buser et al., §2 and Figure 3.

Work through a seam, a boundary and the multiplicity argument

Keep the local equation under reflection and addition.

For an isometry τ, the chain rule and orthogonality give Δ(f∘τ)=(Δf)∘τ. Combining pieces of the same eigenvalue therefore preserves that eigenvalue locally. Combining arbitrary different eigenvalues would not work.

−ΔΣaⱼ(fⱼ∘τⱼ) = λΣaⱼ(fⱼ∘τⱼ)

Check one internal seam: g₀ continues into g₄.

Across edge class a, the source extension rules send f₁→f₀, f₂→f₅ and f₄→−f₄. Consequently the rule in target triangle 0 extends into exactly the rule in adjacent target triangle 4.

g₀ = f₁+f₂+f₄ → f₀+f₅−f₄ = g₄

This is a common local continuation, so values and derivatives in one common normal direction agree across the seam. Merely matching heights would not be enough: |x| is continuous but has a derivative jump at zero.

Check an outer edge using odd reflection.

Across the c-class outer edge of target triangle 4, the source rules send f₀→f₄, f₄→f₀ and f₅→−f₅. The entire target combination becomes its own negative.

g₄ → −g₄; at the fixed reflection edge, g₄=−g₄, so g₄=0

The internal triangle lines are not extra fixed boundaries. The cancellation is required only at the actual outer edge.

Account for all modes, including repeated eigenvalues.

The seven rules form an integer coefficient matrix T on function pieces. Its determinant is 24, so a nonzero input cannot disappear. The signed edge-continuation matrices also satisfy the identities below for each edge class e.

det T = 24 ≠ 0; T A_e = B_e T for all three edge classes

Invertibility together with these continuation identities supplies the reverse correspondence. The resulting map is an isomorphism of eigenspaces for every λ, hence dimensions and multiplicities agree. The determinant alone would not check seams or boundary conditions. The edge matrices are not the membrane’s Laplacian.

Corresponding eigenspaces have the same dimension.

WHAT YOU CAN TAKE AWAY

You can hear the area.
You may still not know
the outline.

Frequency is not the whole recording. A recording also depends on which modes are excited, their amplitudes and how the vibration is measured.

The model matters. This result concerns ideal planar membranes under specified conditions. It is not a claim about every object or every possible measurement.

A QUESTION TO TAKE WITH YOU

What if you knew it was a triangle?

Within the class of triangular drumheads, the complete Dirichlet spectrum does determine the triangle up to congruence. Extra information about the possible shapes can restore uniqueness. [5] The next question is therefore: which geometric features, and which classes of shapes, can we recover?

THE MATHEMATICS / WHAT THE SOUND LEAVES OUT

A spectrum is one part of a motion.

u(x,t) = Σ[aⱼcos(c√λⱼt)+bⱼsin(c√λⱼt)]φⱼ(x)

The domain determines the allowed eigenvalues and eigenspaces. Initial displacement and velocity determine coefficients aⱼ,bⱼ. A measurement also depends on where and how you observe the motion. The website isolates one mode and one pure tone to make the frequency relation clear.

Mathematical tools: eigenfunction expansion · initial conditions · linear superposition

COLLECT THE CORE FORMULASA compact mathematical reference+

A spatial mode φ is an eigenfunction of the negative Laplacian: −Δφ = λφ, with φ = 0 on the boundary. Here Δφ = ∂²φ/∂x² + ∂²φ/∂y² measures local spatial curvature. The wave equation utt = c²Δu relates it to acceleration. Substituting u = φ cos(2πft) gives the eigenvalue equation and the frequency relation below.

f = c√λ / 2π

For a rectangle of side lengths a and b, with positive integers m and n:

φmn(x,y) = sin(mπx/a) sin(nπy/b)
λmn = π²(m²/a² + n²/b²)

Our animated displacement is Aφmn cos(2πfmnt). The wave speed c stays fixed. Doubling a and b divides λ by four and f by two. A single common slowdown is applied to every animated frequency.

For a fixed wave speed c, the eigenvalue list and the frequency list determine each other. “Spectrum” counts repeated eigenvalues with their multiplicities. For example, on a square, the (1, 2) and (2, 1) patterns are independent modes with the same eigenvalue.

The area experiment

For a rectangle, λmn = π²(m²/a² + n²/b²), m,n ≥ 1. Let N(Λ) count all pairs with λmn ≤ Λ, including repeated values. For the positive operator −Δ, Weyl’s law is N(Λ)/Λ → A/(4π). Thus 4πN(Λ)/Λ approaches area A. Counting modes up to a frequency F is equivalent to using Λ = (2πF/c)².

For a bounded smooth planar domain, the heat trace has the first two terms Σe−tλ = A/(4πt) − L/(8√(πt)) + O(1) as t → 0, where L is perimeter. Polygons have the same first two terms, with corner contributions at the constant term. This explains why the complete spectrum fixes both A and L. Kac uses a different operator normalization; the constants here match our −Δ convention. [4]
