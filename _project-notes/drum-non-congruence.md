# Non-congruence comparison — 9 November 2026

The student asked for a page that makes it possible to see why drumheads A and B cannot coincide. The addition is `projects/hear-the-shape/non-congruent.html`, linked from both editions of the essay. It defaults to Chinese and provides an English switch and shareable `?lang=en` URL.

## Reader experience

- Drag A, use position/rotation sliders, or flip it while B stays fixed; scale is held constant.
- Solid teal / dashed orange outlines, a shaded intersection and optional hatching distinguish overlap from the two unmatched parts.
- Toggle construction triangles and the three reflex corners. A guided action restores the unique corner-aligned candidate; a second action marks a tip of A outside B.
- The displayed percentage is intersection area divided by the area of one drumhead, for the current pose. It is not asserted to be a global maximum. The central alignment has exactly four common triangles: 4/7, displayed as 57.1%.
- Keyboard controls, labelled range inputs, mobile layout, reduced-motion handling and an illustrated no-JavaScript fallback are included.

## Mathematics

Geometry is generated by reflection from the same acute scalene triangle, vertices (0,0), (1.4,0), (.45,1), and the same edge-color adjacency rules already used in the essay. Generated coordinates were checked against all fourteen triangles in the existing published SVG assets.

Each boundary has nine vertices and exactly three reflex vertices. Those three vertices are the corners of the central triangle. Their squared pairwise distances, multiplied by 400, are 784, 761 and 481. The lengths are distinct, so only one of the six corner correspondences preserves every pairwise distance. Three non-collinear point correspondences determine a planar rigid motion, including the possibility of reflection. For this pair the candidate aligns the central triangles; an outer tip of A lies outside B. Thus the candidate does not identify the whole domains, and no other candidate can do so.

This is the geometric/non-isometry argument, distinct from the analytic transplantation proof of isospectrality. Both are needed for an isospectral non-isometric counterexample. Source: Buser, Conway, Doyle & Semmler, *Some planar isospectral domains*, 1994 version, section 2 and Figures 2–3: https://math.dartmouth.edu/~doyle/docs/drum/drum.pdf.
