Theoretical computer science

The ambition is a precise theorem: an algorithmic guarantee, a resource bound, or a structural characterization. Each direction would first need a focused literature review and a narrower problem statement.

01Research proposal

Spectral graph algorithms

Preserve the graph.
Keep fewer edges.

Resistance-Aware Construction of High-fidelity Edge-sparse Laplacians

Can a well-defined structural property of a graph make resistance-based sparsification cheaper, while retaining a rigorous spectral approximation?

Initial scope. Start with connected, weighted, undirected graphs with positive edge weights. Study one explicitly specified graph family and one source of cost, such as estimating effective resistances. The sparse output would keep the same vertices and may reweight retained edges.

Guarantee to preserve

\[(1-\varepsilon)L_G \preceq L_H \preceq (1+\varepsilon)L_G\]

For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), every vector's Laplacian quadratic form is preserved within relative error \(\varepsilon\). This is the standard target definition, not a new result.

Proposed approach & intended contribution

Method to investigate

Use effective resistance \(R_e=b_e^\mathsf{T}L_G^{+}b_e\), where \(b_e\) is an edge's signed incidence vector and \(L_G^{+}\) is the pseudoinverse. Begin with the established sampling baseline based on \(w_eR_e\), then investigate whether structure permits cheaper resistance estimates or a sharper analysis. Track edge count, running time, error, and failure probability.

First milestone

Reproduce the baseline proof and implementation. On small graphs, compare Laplacian quadratic forms and generalized eigenvalues on the nonconstant subspace. Numerical checks would help find counterexamples; they would not establish the guarantee.

Target contribution

A proved improvement for a specified graph family or parameter regime, if one can be found, together with reproducible experiments. The spectral inequality itself is already known; novelty would have to lie in a verified improvement over an appropriate baseline.

Starting point: Spielman & Srivastava, Graph Sparsification by Effective Resistances.

02Research proposal

Streaming algorithms & lower bounds

Count local structure.
Understand the memory cost.

Randomized Approximate Counting with High-probability Error Limits

Graph motif estimation under limited memory

How much memory is needed to estimate triangles or other fixed small subgraphs in an edge stream, and which structural parameters determine that cost?

Initial scope. Begin with one pass over an insertion-only stream of a simple, undirected graph in adversarial order, with each edge appearing once. Fix the motif and estimation time. Random-order streams, deletions, and extra passes would be separate models.

Target accuracy, under stated assumptions

\[\Pr\!\left[|\widehat T-T|\leq\varepsilon T\right]\geq1-\delta\]

Here \(T>0\) is the motif count and \(\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)\). Zero or very small counts need an explicit promise, detection guarantee, or additive-error formulation.

Proposed approach & intended contribution

Method to investigate

Start with reservoir or wedge sampling, analyze unbiasedness and variance, and identify how motif overlap affects concentration. Compare space and update time at the same error and confidence levels. For a lower bound, investigate a communication-complexity reduction that preserves the chosen stream model, graph promises, and number of passes.

First milestone

Reproduce a triangle-counting baseline on graph families with different overlap patterns. Write a parameter table that states the number of vertices and edges, any count promise, space in bits, and failure probability. Empirical accuracy would be reported separately from proved guarantees.

Target contribution

A space upper bound under explicit structural assumptions, or a lower bound explaining why a desired memory budget is impossible. A matching bound is an aspiration, not an expected result. Extension to other motifs would follow only after the triangle case is understood.

Starting point: TRIÈST, with careful attention to which variant matches the chosen stream model.

03Research proposal

Computational learning theory

Separate representation
from learnability.

Representation-Aware Characterization of Hypothesis Expressivity and Learnability

How do explicit restrictions on a hypothesis representation change the functions it can express, the samples it needs, and the computation required to learn?

Initial scope. Choose one restricted Boolean-function or sequence-model family. Define the input encoding, size or depth, numerical precision, and learning model before making any claim. Restricted Transformer architectures are a possible later specialization.

Expressivity

What can the class represent?

Sample complexity

How much data is sufficient or necessary?

Computation

Can learning be efficient?

Proposed approach & intended contribution

Method to investigate

Use constructions and separating examples for expressivity. For a binary class, investigate VC dimension or covering arguments for sample bounds under a stated PAC setting. Distinguish realizable from agnostic learning, and proper from improper learners. Explore computational reductions only after fixing the learner's access to examples or queries.

First milestone

Define a small family precisely and reproduce its known expressivity and learning bounds. Seek one informative separation between representation sizes or restrictions, with a complete proof or an explicit counterexample to the initial conjecture.

Target contribution

A structural theorem, a sample-complexity bound, or a computational hardness result with every assumption stated. Expressing a function does not by itself imply efficient learning; any conditional lower bound would be labeled with its complexity or cryptographic assumption.

Starting point: Kearns & Valiant on cryptographic limitations of learning.

Secondary directions / Exploratory proposals

Exploratory AI Research Ideas

Five adjacent questions to revisit as the theoretical foundations develop. These are possible studies, not implemented systems or claims of demonstrated capability.

AMathematical conjecture discoveryCan counterexamples help a system ask better mathematical questions?

Proposed method. Restrict exploration to a small mathematical domain. Combine conjecture generation, counterexample search, and attempts at formal proof; compare against the same generator without feedback.

Evaluation & goal. Measure formal validity, counterexample rates, duplication, and expert-assessed interest on held-out tasks. Aim for a reproducible benchmark and an analysis of feedback's effect. Formal validity alone would not establish novelty or mathematical importance.

BContinual agentsCan experience improve future behavior without erasing earlier skills?

Proposed method. Compare episodic memory, retrieval, and controlled learning updates across a fixed sequence of tasks, including a no-memory baseline.

Evaluation & goal. Measure retention, transfer to new tasks, repeated mistakes, and resource use. Aim for an evaluation protocol that distinguishes recalled solutions from newly learned behavior, and tests whether improvements persist after the task distribution changes.

CCausal hypothesis discoveryCan a system propose hypotheses that survive an intervention?

Proposed method. Combine literature-grounded hypothesis generation with explicit causal models and experiment selection. Start in synthetic environments where interventions and ground truth are available.

Evaluation & goal. Assess intervention predictions, experiment efficiency, source attribution, and sensitivity to hidden confounding. Aim for a traceable hypothesis-to-test workflow. Observational associations would not be presented as established causal effects.

DReliable reasoningCan uncertainty identify the first unsupported step in a mathematical argument?

Proposed method. Evaluate step-level verification and confidence estimates on mathematical solutions with human-checked or formally checked error labels, including plausible but incorrect derivations.

Evaluation & goal. Measure error localization, calibration, and accuracy when the system can abstain, including on unfamiliar problems. Aim for a transparent reliability benchmark; a confident explanation would not count as evidence of correctness.

ESelf-improving codingCan verified repair feedback improve performance on genuinely new bugs?

Proposed method. Study bounded cycles of diagnosis, patch generation, testing, and feedback on isolated benchmark repositories. Keep evaluation tests hidden and separate training feedback from held-out tasks.

Evaluation & goal. Measure repair success, regressions, test overfitting, cost, and transfer across repositories. Aim for a reproducible account of when feedback helps; passing visible tests alone would not establish program correctness or general self-improvement.

From an idea to a research question

A modest, testable beginning.

  1. Read & delimitMap known results and choose one precise model.
  2. ReproduceUnderstand a baseline proof and its limitations.
  3. Conjecture & testLook for a small improvement or a decisive counterexample.
  4. Prove & documentState assumptions, evidence, and what remains unresolved.
